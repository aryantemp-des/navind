# Navya Tech Industry — Website Structure

## Purpose

This document defines the information architecture, page hierarchy, navigation model, and content structure for the Navya Tech Industry website.

It is specifically written for Antigravity.

Use this together with `base_information.md`.

- `base_information.md` = what Navya Tech Industry is
- `structure.md` = how the website information should be organized
- Future documents can separately define design, technical implementation, content, and QA

---

# 1. Core Website Objective

The Navya Tech Industry website must present NTI as a **professional technology company with the scale and maturity of an international technology organization**, not as a conventional creative agency.

The website should communicate that NTI operates across:

- Digital experiences
- Web development
- UI/UX
- Interactive and immersive technology
- AI agents
- Workflow automation
- Business systems
- Integrations
- Performance
- SEO and digital growth

The architecture must be scalable enough for NTI to grow into a multinational organization without requiring a complete restructuring of the website.

---

# 2. Strategic Positioning

The website should position NTI as:

> **A technology company that builds digital experiences people use and intelligent systems businesses rely on.**

The website should communicate two connected sides of NTI.

## Customer-facing technology

- Websites
- Digital products
- UI/UX
- Interactive experiences
- 3D
- Web applications

## Business-facing technology

- AI agents
- Workflow automation
- Business process automation
- Integrations
- Automated communications
- Intelligent business systems
- Data processing
- Automated reporting

These must feel like parts of one technology company, not unrelated services.

---

# 3. MNC / CORPORATE POSITIONING

The website must **not** feel like a small freelance agency or generic design studio.

Avoid structuring the website around:

- Generic agency packages
- Excessive portfolio thumbnails
- Pricing cards for every service
- "Let's make magic" language
- Generic startup language
- Artificially futuristic AI branding
- Excessive decorative effects
- Empty corporate buzzwords

Instead communicate:

- Capability
- Scale
- Reliability
- Technical depth
- Business understanding
- Process maturity
- Operational capability
- Long-term partnership
- International readiness
- Professionalism

The website should feel capable of becoming the public digital presence of a large technology organization.

# 51. Rules for Antigravity

When implementing this structure:

1. Treat this document as the **information architecture source of truth**.
2. Treat `base_information.md` as the **company-context source of truth**.
3. Do not invent company facts.
4. Do not invent clients, metrics, offices, awards, employees, partnerships, or results.
5. Keep the primary navigation concise.
6. Do not expose every capability directly in the navbar.
7. Preserve the distinction between Capabilities, Solutions, and Work.
8. Build reusable structures without making every page visually identical.
9. Make the architecture scalable for future MNC expansion.
10. Do not create future sections merely to make NTI appear larger than it is.
11. Prioritize real content and real evidence.
12. Keep customer-facing experiences and business-facing automation under one coherent identity.
13. Do not turn the website into an agency portfolio.
14. Do not turn the website into an AI-startup landing page.
15. Do not turn the website into a framework showcase.
16. Do not overload the homepage with every service.
17. Do not create unnecessary pages.
18. Every page must have a clear informational or business purpose.
19. If content is missing, use a clearly identifiable placeholder or leave the section out rather than inventing facts.
20. The website should be designed for the organization NTI is building toward, while remaining truthful about the organization it is today.

---

# 52. Professional Technology Company vs Agency

The website should communicate:

### NOT

> "We make cool websites."

### BUT

> "We provide technology capabilities that help organizations build digital experiences and intelligent business systems."

The website should feel like the public interface of a **technology organization**, not the portfolio of a freelancer or boutique creative agency.

---

# 53. Structural Quality Test

Before considering the structure complete, verify:

- Can a first-time visitor understand NTI quickly?
- Can a potential client understand what NTI actually does?
- Can a technical decision-maker understand NTI's capabilities?
- Can a business owner understand the business value?
- Can someone looking for automation find it easily?
- Can someone looking for web development find it easily?
- Can visitors see evidence of previous work?
- Can visitors understand how NTI works?
- Is there a clear route to starting a project?
- Can the architecture scale as NTI becomes larger?
- Does the website feel like a technology company rather than an agency?
- Is every page justified by a real purpose?

If any answer is no, revise the information architecture before adding visual complexity.

---

# 54. Final Structural Principle

The website should communicate this progression:

```text
WHO WE ARE
     ↓
WHAT WE CAN DO
     ↓
WHAT BUSINESS PROBLEMS WE SOLVE
     ↓
WHAT WE HAVE BUILT
     ↓
HOW WE WORK
     ↓
WHY WE ARE TRUSTWORTHY
     ↓
HOW TO WORK WITH US
```

The website is not supposed to show everything NTI can possibly do.

It is supposed to make the right visitor understand:

> **What Navya Tech Industry is, what it can build, what problems it can solve, why it is credible, and how to engage with it.**

That is the structural foundation for a professional, scalable, MNC-oriented Navya Tech Industry website.
